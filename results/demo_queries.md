**Q.** What does a governing-law clause usually decide?

Answered from general knowledge, not from your documents.

A **governing-law clause** (also called a **choice-of-law clause**) typically decides the following:

1. **Which jurisdiction's law applies** – It specifies the body of law (e.g., the law of a particular state or country) that will be used to interpret, enforce, and resolve disputes arising from the contract.

2. **Interpretation of contract terms** – The chosen law governs how ambiguous or disputed contract language is construed.

3. **Validity and enforceability** – It determines whether the contract (or specific provisions within it) is legally valid and enforceable.

4. **Rights and obligations of the parties** – It establishes which legal rules define what each party must do or is entitled to under the agreement.

5. **Remedies available** – It influences what remedies (damages, specific performance, etc.) are available in the event of a breach.

**What it generally does NOT decide:**
- **Jurisdiction or venue** for disputes (that is typically handled by a separate *forum-selection clause*).
- Procedural rules of any court or arbitration panel hearing the dispute.
- Certain mandatory laws (e.g., consumer protection or employment laws) that may apply regardless of the parties' choice.

In short, a governing-law clause primarily determines the **substantive legal rules** that control the contract's meaning, performance, and enforcement.
